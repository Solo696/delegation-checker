// What is wallet delegation?
🔑 What Is Delegation?
Think of your wallet like a house. Delegation is like handing a stranger a master key — they can now enter, take your stuff, and even change the locks so you can't get back in. You might have handed them that key without even realising it.
🚨 How You Get Hacked
Scammers create fake websites that look real — like a popular crypto app or airdrop page. When you click "Sign" on what looks like a harmless login request, you're actually handing them control of your wallet. No password, no gas fee needed on your end.
🩹 How to Remove It
If your wallet is flagged here, open Ambire or Frame wallet and look for a "clear delegation" option. Act fast — do not send or approve anything until it's cleared. If you're stuck, contact SEAL 911 below.
🛡️ How to Stay Safe
Never click "Sign" on popups from sites you don't fully trust. If a site asks you to sign something to "verify your wallet" or "claim your airdrop" — that's a red flag. When in doubt, close the tab and check the official site URL first.
// How the scam happens — step by step
1
You land on a fake website

Scammers build websites that look identical to real crypto apps — a popular DEX, an NFT drop, a token airdrop page. The logo is right, the design looks polished. You'd never know it's fake just by looking.

2
A popup asks you to "sign" something

Your wallet pops up and asks you to sign a message. It might say "Verify your wallet", "Claim your reward", or "Connect to continue." It looks exactly like the harmless login prompts you sign all the time — and it costs you no gas fee, so nothing feels off.

3
That signature is actually handing over your wallet

Hidden in that "harmless" signature is a secret instruction that gives the scammer full control of your wallet. You can't see it in plain text — it's buried in data your wallet doesn't warn you about yet.

4
The scammer drains you — without needing anything else from you

The scammer takes your signature and uses it themselves. Your wallet now obeys their commands. They can drain your tokens, steal your NFTs, or block you from using your own wallet — all without you doing anything else.

5
You try to fix it — but you're locked out

When you realise something's wrong and try to take back control, the scammer's code fights back. It blocks your attempts, or the scammer just re-locks your wallet faster than you can free it. This is why acting immediately matters.

// My wallet is compromised — what do I do right now?
🚨 Stop everything first
Do NOT send any tokens. Do NOT approve any transactions. Do NOT connect to any site. Close all browser tabs. Then work through the steps below.
STEP 1 — DO THIS YOURSELF
Open Ambire Wallet (ambire.com) or Frame Wallet (frame.sh) — both are free. Look for a "Delegation" or "Account" settings section and select Clear Delegation or Revoke. This works for most people without needing any outside help.
STEP 2 — PROTECT WHAT'S LEFT
If you can't clear it yourself, don't waste time — create a brand new wallet immediately using MetaMask or Trust Wallet. Write down the new seed phrase on paper, keep it offline. Then move any remaining tokens to that new wallet. Never go back to the compromised one.
⚠ IMPORTANT WARNING
Never accept help from strangers online — anyone who DMs you offering to "recover your wallet" or "reverse the hack" is a scammer running a second attack on you. No legitimate person will ever ask for your seed phrase, private key, or remote access to your device. If someone does — block them immediately.
